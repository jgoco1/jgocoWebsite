Datapacks

Nine standalone Minecraft datapacks I've made, built for 1.21.x (data pack format matching Minecraft 26.2). Each one is self-contained — drop any combination into your world's datapacks folder and run /reload. No dependencies between them except where noted.

TNT / Campfire / Blaze / Concrete Recipe Tweaks

Download .zip

Bundle of small recipe changes aimed at cutting down on tedious early/mid-game grinding:

  • TNT — now a shapeless recipe: 1 sand or red sand + 1 gunpowder (down from the vanilla checkerboard pattern of 4 sand + 5 gunpowder).
  • Campfire — 1 log (any type) + 1 stick, instead of 3 sticks + 3 logs + coal/charcoal.
  • Blaze rods — now yield 4 blaze powder instead of 2.
  • Concrete powder — all 16 colors reduced to 1 sand + 1 gravel + 1 dye, yielding 16 concrete powder per craft (up from 4 sand + 4 gravel → 8).

No new mechanics, just friendlier ratios on things that felt needlessly expensive.

Fishing Treasure Boost

Download .zip

Reworks the vanilla fishing loot table:

  • Treasure catch rate raised to a flat 50% (junk and fish odds adjusted to compensate).
  • Luck of the Sea now scales how many items you pull up per reel, similar in spirit to how Fortune/Looting scale drop counts. It works by stacking several "bonus catch" rolls on top of the base one, each gated behind the enchantment's actual level on your rod — so a level 3 rod nets you multiple items most casts, not just better odds on a single item.

Fully vanilla-compatible — overrides the same loot table any other pack touching fishing would use, so it also affects the barrel pack below.

Fishing Barrels

Download .zip

Passive fish farming, hands-off. Place a barrel touching water and it'll periodically roll the same fishing loot table above and deposit the result directly into itself — junk, fish, and treasure included.

  • Only needs 1 adjacent water block (flowing or source) to activate at all — and the more of its 6 sides touch water, the faster it produces. A single water block behaves like the old baseline (~10–30s per catch); fully submerged on all sides cuts that down to roughly 1.7–5s.
  • Water is re-measured every cycle, so knocking a side out or adding more adjusts the pace immediately rather than needing to be reset.
  • New barrels are detected automatically the moment they're placed. Barrels already in the world before installing need a one-time /function fishing_barrels:init to activate (a reminder prints when the pack loads).

Campfire Aura

Download .zip

Turns lit campfires and soul campfires into small support auras:

  • Regeneration to players and allied mobs (villagers, wandering traders, allays) within 8 blocks.
  • Iron golems get a wider radius — 16 blocks — on the theory that a golem guarding a base benefits more from being reachable at range than from precisely matching the player radius.
  • 2 damage per second to hostile mobs within 16 blocks (uses a damage type that bypasses armor).
  • Place the campfire on top of a hay bale (the same setup that triggers vanilla's tall "signal fire" smoke) and all radii double (16/32/32).
  • Extinguishing pauses the effect; breaking it removes it entirely — both detected automatically.

New campfires are picked up the moment they're placed. Ones already in the world need a one-time /function campfire_aura:init.

Flexible Enchants

Download .zip

Three changes to the enchanting/anvil system, aimed at "why can't I just have the build I want":

  • Removes several vanilla mutual-exclusivity restrictions — Protection / Fire Protection / Blast Protection / Projectile Protection can all coexist on one armor piece; Infinity + Mending on the same bow; Depth Strider + Frost Walker on the same boots; Density/Breach on a mace; Multishot + Piercing on a crossbow. (Fortune/Silk Touch, Loyalty/Riptide/Channeling, and the Sharpness/Smite/Bane of Arthropods family are left alone on purpose — see note below.)
  • Drops the anvil cost of ~20 enchantments to the practical minimum — Mending, Infinity, the Protection line, Unbreaking, and several others no longer eat disproportionate levels to apply.
  • Eliminates the anvil's "prior work penalty" — the cost that normally doubles every time an item's touched an anvil, eventually hitting the "Too Expensive!" wall. This pack clears that penalty automatically the moment you open an anvil, so repeated combining stays cheap indefinitely.

Note: a handful of enchantments (Efficiency, Aqua Affinity, Depth Strider, Respiration, Sweeping Edge, Swift Sneak, Soul Speed, Sharpness, Smite, Bane of Arthropods, and a few others) are intentionally left at vanilla cost — some reference parts of the game's internals I couldn't fully verify for this version, so they were left untouched rather than risk breaking world loading.

Furnace Quarry

Download .zip

Passive stone/ore generation using nothing but placement and plumbing — no mining required. Place a blast furnace (or a regular furnace, for a weaker version) so that it sits between lava and water — a source block of each, on opposite sides along any axis — with a chest, barrel, hopper, trapped chest, or copper chest adjacent to it. It'll periodically deposit stone, deepslate, andesite/diorite/granite, or ores directly into that container.

  • Loot odds scale with the furnace's actual Y-level, mimicking real ore distribution: diamond, redstone, and gold get much more common the deeper you go, iron peaks around Y16, and copper/coal are most common near the surface. Every material is still possible at every depth, just weighted differently.
  • Blast furnace: full ore table, produces roughly every 15–30 seconds.
  • Regular furnace: restricted to stone/coal/copper/iron only, and far slower — about a 1.5% chance each second, so it's a background trickle rather than a real production line.
  • Same placement-detection and one-time /function furnace_quarry:init pattern as the other tracked-block packs above.

Mining Speed Boost

Download .zip

One flat, permanent buff: +11 mining speed added to every player's mining_efficiency attribute, applied automatically the first time they're seen in the world.

Calibrated so an Efficiency V diamond pickaxe instantly mines stone (the vanilla threshold is a mining-speed value of 45; a fully-enchanted diamond pickaxe alone caps out at 34 — this closes that exact gap). A flat bonus rather than gear-scaled, so it speeds up mining across the board, not just at max enchant level.

The bonus now survives death — it's re-verified once a second and silently reapplied if it's ever missing, working around a Minecraft quirk where custom attribute modifiers (unlike most other player data) don't persist through respawn.

Stonecutter Splitting

Download .zip

Three stonecutter recipes for turning "too much of one thing" into "useful amounts of something else":

  • 1 Stone → 2 Cobblestone
  • 1 Cobblestone → 2 Gravel
  • 1 Gravel → 2 Sand

Useful for a sand/gravel-heavy build (concrete, TNT, etc.) without a trip back to a beach or desert.

Includes an unlock advancement so these actually show up in the stonecutter's recipe list — unlike a crafting table, the stonecutter has no "manually arrange items" fallback, so a new recipe needs to be explicitly unlocked to be usable at all, not just to appear highlighted.

Craftable Villager Spawn Egg

Download .zip

Adds a shaped crafting recipe for a Villager Spawn Egg — normally a creative-only item: 4 Emeralds surrounding any Bed. Thematically it lines up with vanilla villager mechanics (emeralds as currency, a bed as the thing a villager actually needs to move in) rather than being an arbitrary cost.

Since this is a new recipe rather than an override of an existing one, it won't show up highlighted in the in-game recipe book automatically — it crafts correctly regardless, but there's no automatic "unlock" notification. (This is different from the stonecutter recipes above — crafting-table recipes work without an unlock advancement, stonecutter recipes don't.)
